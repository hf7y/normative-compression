# Predictions and Falsification Criteria

The project should publish these criteria before confirmatory analysis.

## Positive evidence

- A reproducible association between classificatory centrality and normative conclusions;
- reduced explicit normative premises in high-normativity classifications;
- boundary policing associated with preservation of downstream normative inference;
- robustness across samples and reasonable operationalizations.

## Disconfirming evidence

- Compression is equally strong in neutral controls;
- boundary policing does not predict downstream normative preservation;
- results disappear under genre/length controls;
- independent annotators cannot reliably identify the relevant components;
- compression metrics do not predict any theoretically relevant outcome;
- an alternative model explains the data with fewer assumptions.

## Strong falsification

The hypothesis should be considered seriously weakened if a preregistered, balanced corpus finds no effect across multiple reasonable operationalizations and the expected boundary-policing interaction is absent.
