# Research Program

## Normative Compression and the Engineering of Concepts

This repository is the research laboratory for a long-form project on conceptual engineering. The immediate empirical question is whether normative conclusions can become compressed into classificatory concepts, but the larger program asks whether conceptual engineering can be reconstructed as an empirically testable theory of representational transformation.

## The larger architecture

The program has four layers:

1. **Philosophical foundations** — Carnap, Rorty, contemporary conceptual engineering, inferentialism, conceptual ethics, amelioration, replacement, uptake, and implementation.
2. **Engineering theory** — a vocabulary of conceptual objects, operations, target states, constraints, failure modes, and performance criteria.
3. **Formal representation** — argument graphs, information-theoretic ideas, compression, distortion, coding, filtering, reconstruction, and signal-processing models, tested rather than assumed.
4. **Empirical and intervention programs** — balanced corpora, annotation, benchmark arguments with known ground truth, quantitative measurement, comparative cases, and eventually deliberate conceptual interventions.

## Core research questions

### RQ1 — Phenomenology
Do conceptual systems exhibit normative compression?

### RQ2 — Mechanism
Does boundary policing preserve compressed inferential structures?

### RQ3 — Formalization
Can compression be represented mathematically without merely redescribing the phenomenon metaphorically?

### RQ4 — Measurement
Can the relevant operations be detected and measured reliably in natural language?

### RQ5 — Generalization
Does the phenomenon recur across conceptual domains, genres, disciplines, and political positions?

### RQ6 — Intervention
Can conceptual transformations be deliberately engineered and their effects measured?

### RQ7 — Philosophy
What would an empirical theory of conceptual engineering imply for Rorty's account of vocabulary, contingency, and normative justification?

## The governing methodological principle

> The corpus and formal model must be capable of showing that normative compression does not exist, that it is an artifact of the operationalization, or that it is not distinctive to conceptual engineering.

A preferred interpretation is not an annotation category. Observable components must be annotated independently, and compression must be calculated from them.

## Distinguish the hypotheses

The project contains at least two independent hypotheses:

- **H1: normative compression** — classificatory concepts can reduce the explicit inferential distance between empirical description and normative conclusion.
- **H2: formal adequacy** — signal-processing, information-theoretic, or graph-theoretic concepts provide a valid and useful formal model of that compression.

H1 may survive if H2 fails. A failure of the signal-processing formalism is therefore a substantive result, not a failed project.

## Eventual benchmark

The project should develop a benchmark set containing synthetic and naturally occurring examples with expert-annotated ground truth for:

- explicit normative reasoning;
- ordinary descriptive classification;
- definitional compression;
- normative compression;
- boundary policing;
- counterexample-driven reclassification;
- conceptual engineering;
- superficially similar but non-engineering cases.

The benchmark is intended to test whether a proposed metric reproduces distinctions that are known independently of the metric.

## Eventual intervention program

Once the descriptive and formal models are validated, the program can move from analysis to design. A deliberately engineered conceptual transformation can be evaluated for:

- inferential recall;
- information loss;
- ambiguity;
- uptake;
- normative judgment;
- robustness to counterexamples;
- stability across audiences.

This is the point at which conceptual engineering would become engineering in a stronger, interventionist sense rather than merely borrowing the term metaphorically.
