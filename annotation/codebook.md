# Annotation Codebook — Draft

The annotation scheme must mark observable components rather than asking annotators to judge the theory.

## Core fields

### EMPIRICAL_PREMISE
A proposition presented as factual, historical, descriptive, or otherwise non-normative evidence.

### NORMATIVE_PREMISE
A proposition expressing an evaluative, prescriptive, rights-based, justice-based, or obligation-generating principle.

### CLASSIFICATION
An explicit assignment of a person, group, event, territory, or object to a category.

### CLASSIFICATION_CRITERION
A property invoked to determine category membership.

### NORMATIVE_CONCLUSION
A proposition asserting what ought to be done, what rights/status are owed, or what moral/political consequence follows.

### DEFINITIONAL_LANGUAGE
Language such as 'means', 'refers to', 'properly called', 'by definition', 'should be reserved for'.

### BOUNDARY_CHALLENGE
A case or argument that tests whether an entity belongs in a category.

### BOUNDARY_POLICING
An explicit or implicit response that excludes, reclassifies, or narrows a category boundary.

### RECLASSIFICATION
An entity previously associated with a category is assigned to another category or denied membership.

### INFERENTIAL_LINK
An explicit or recoverable relation connecting two claims.

## Prohibited annotation shortcut

Do not annotate 'NORMATIVE_COMPRESSION = yes/no' directly. A derived measure may be computed later from independently annotated components.

## Confidence

Each nontrivial annotation should have a confidence value and, where practical, a brief rationale. Ambiguous cases should be retained rather than silently discarded.
