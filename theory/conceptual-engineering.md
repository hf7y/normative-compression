# Conceptual Engineering

This project treats conceptual engineering as a family of practices in which representational resources are deliberately evaluated, revised, introduced, stabilized, or policed in light of purposes and inferential consequences.

## Relevant traditions

### Carnapian explication

A defective or vague concept can be replaced by a more precise concept designed for specified purposes. The project inherits the emphasis on purpose and performance, while asking how performance can be measured empirically.

### Ameliorative analysis

Haslanger's work is especially relevant because social concepts can be evaluated partly by what they enable us to understand and change. This makes normative objectives legitimate inputs into conceptual design, but does not eliminate the need to justify the objectives or evaluate alternatives.

### Inferentialism

Concepts can be analyzed through the inferential relations they support. This is central to the present project: the object of engineering may be not merely a category's extension but its inferential profile.

### Implementation and uptake

A proposed concept does not automatically become socially effective. Engineering therefore raises questions about promulgation, uptake, stabilization, institutionalization, and resistance.

## Research gap

The project asks whether conceptual engineering can be studied as an empirical process with measurable transformations in discourse. In particular, it proposes testing whether some engineered concepts compress normative arguments and whether boundary policing helps maintain that compression.

## Rortyan connection

Rorty's anti-foundationalism motivates a methodological stance rather than a premise about the corpus: researchers should not assume that a vocabulary-neutral philosophical foundation determines the correct normative classification. Competing vocabularies can be studied in terms of the distinctions, inferences, and social purposes they enable.

This does not imply that every engineered concept is equally good. The project distinguishes:

1. empirical description of existing usage;
2. reconstruction of conceptual engineering;
3. normative evaluation of an engineered concept.

Those questions must not be collapsed.
