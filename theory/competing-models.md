# Competing Models

The project should not assume that signal-processing compression is the correct explanation.

## Model A — Normative compression

A classifier incorporates or activates normative information, reducing explicit inferential distance to a normative conclusion.

## Model B — Ordinary argument compression

All academic discourse abbreviates arguments. No special normative mechanism is required.

## Model C — Descriptive compression

Classifiers naturally encode descriptive information; apparent normative compression is a byproduct of ordinary categorization.

## Model D — Genre effect

Philosophical, legal, or advocacy genres have distinctive argument structures that produce apparent compression independently of conceptual engineering.

## Model E — Advocacy effect

Compression is associated with motivated advocacy rather than with conceptual engineering as such.

## Model F — Term-specific effect

The phenomenon is unusually strong for Indigenous discourse but does not generalize to other normative classifications.

## Model G — Symmetric political engineering

Competing political communities may engineer opposing classificatory boundaries with comparable compression. If so, the mechanism is general rather than politically one-sided.

## Model H — Boundary-policing artifact

What looks like conceptual engineering is actually ordinary definitional dispute or No True Scotsman-style exclusion, with no special relationship to downstream normative conclusions.

Every confirmatory analysis should state which models it distinguishes and which it cannot distinguish.
