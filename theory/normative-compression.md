# Normative Compression

## Working definition

**Normative compression** is the hypothesized phenomenon in which a classificatory representation incorporates, presupposes, or conventionally activates information that would otherwise need to appear as explicit normative premises in an argument.

A simple uncompressed architecture is:

`empirical premises → classification → independent normative principle → normative conclusion`

A compressed architecture is:

`empirical/classificatory representation → normative conclusion`

The central question is not whether the latter is logically valid. It is whether the missing argumentative material has been **encoded in the classifier** or its established inferential role.

## Signal-processing hypothesis

A long argument can be represented as a structured signal containing descriptive and normative information. A classificatory term may act as an encoding device that transmits a reduced representation while allowing downstream readers to reconstruct a preferred inference.

Candidate abstraction:

`CR = inferential complexity(expanded argument) / inferential complexity(compressed argument)`

This ratio is provisional. The project must determine whether inferential complexity can be defined reliably and whether information-theoretic compression is actually the right model.

## What would count as evidence?

Evidence for compression would require more than a normative word being present. Strong evidence would involve:

- identifiable empirical/descriptive premises;
- a classificatory term that sits between those premises and a normative conclusion;
- normative premises that are absent, implicit, or encoded in the classification;
- explicit or implicit boundary rules that preserve the desired inference;
- adversarial cases in which expanding the category would weaken the downstream inference.

## What would count against it?

- Equivalent compression in normatively neutral classifications;
- no association between boundary policing and preservation of normative inferences;
- apparent compression disappearing when genre, document type, or argument length is controlled;
- reliable annotation proving impossible;
- alternative graph measures explaining the data equally well without the compression construct.

## Important distinction

Compression is not synonymous with:

- vagueness;
- rhetorical persuasion;
- definitional circularity;
- No True Scotsman;
- ordinary abbreviation;
- normative bias.

Those may be related phenomena or alternative explanations. The research must distinguish them empirically.
