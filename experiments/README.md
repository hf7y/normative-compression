# Experiments

## Phase 0 — Synthetic validation

Construct controlled arguments with known degrees and types of compression. Include:

- uncompressed arguments;
- descriptive compression;
- normative compression;
- definitional compression;
- boundary-policing cases;
- adversarial cases that look compressed but are not.

The purpose is to test whether proposed representations and metrics recover known structure before natural-language corpus analysis.

## Phase 1 — Human validation

Use blinded annotation and independent adjudication to establish whether trained annotators can reliably identify the observable components from which the metric is calculated.

## Phase 2 — Pilot corpus

Test extraction, annotation, graph construction, and measurement on a deliberately small corpus. This phase is for debugging the research instrument, not confirming the hypothesis.

## Phase 3 — Preregistered corpus study

Freeze sampling rules, annotation rules, primary metrics, controls, and statistical tests before confirmatory analysis.

## Phase 4 — Comparative replication

Test whether findings generalize across terms, disciplines, genres, periods, and opposed political positions.

## Phase 5 — Intervention

If the descriptive and formal models survive validation, deliberately engineer alternative conceptual formulations and measure their effects.
