# Normative Compression

An empirical research program on conceptual engineering, inferential compression, and the discourse of normative classification.

## Research question

> Can normative conclusions become compressed into classificatory concepts in ways that measurably reduce the explicit argumentative distance between empirical descriptions and normative conclusions—and does boundary policing function to preserve that compression?

This repository is deliberately organized as a **falsifiable research program**, not as a case for a predetermined conclusion. The corpus and models should be capable of showing that normative compression is weak, absent, genre-specific, term-specific, or an artifact of the operationalization.

## Core hypothesis

A classificatory concept can function as an inferential macro: information needed to support a normative conclusion may be encoded in the concept's extension, criteria, or conventional inferential role. When an adversarial case threatens the downstream inference, speakers may police the category boundary rather than reopen the normative argument.

Proposed architecture:

`normative objective → conceptual compression → boundary policing → naturalized classification → apparently immediate conclusion`

This is a hypothesis about a mechanism, not an assumption about any particular author or political position.

## Research architecture

- **bibliography/** — literature that licenses and constrains the methodology
- **theory/** — conceptual engineering, Rorty, inferentialism, compression, competing models
- **hypotheses/** — primary, alternative, null, and falsification criteria
- **corpus/** — sampling, sources, metadata, inclusion/exclusion rules
- **annotation/** — observable annotation scheme, examples, agreement, adjudication
- **methods/** — CADS/corpus analysis, claim extraction, argument structure, metrics, statistics
- **models/** — formal representations and simulations
- **analysis/** — exploratory and confirmatory analyses and figures
- **experiments/** — pilot studies, preregistration, replication
- **docs/** — research plan, glossary, decision log, kill-the-hypothesis file

## Methodological commitments

1. **Balanced corpus over cherry-picking.** Sampling decisions precede result inspection where feasible.
2. **Observable annotation over theory-laden labels.** Annotators mark premises, classifications, conclusions, definitions, boundary challenges, and reclassifications rather than simply judging whether a passage exhibits compression.
3. **Competing hypotheses are first-class.** The project actively seeks explanations that do not require normative compression.
4. **Separate exploration from confirmation.** Discovery may generate hypotheses; confirmatory analysis tests preregistered operationalizations.
5. **Model the metaphor rather than assume it.** Signal-processing language is useful only if formal measures and graphs demonstrate explanatory value.
6. **Keep empirical semantics separate from normative evaluation.** What speakers do with a concept, what a concept ought to mean, and whether the resulting norm is justified are distinct questions.

## Status

Research design / repository scaffold. No substantive corpus claims have been established yet.

## Immediate research sequence

1. Literature autopsy: conceptual engineering, inferentialism, ameliorative analysis, implementation/uptake, corpus methods, argument mining, information theory.
2. Formalize candidate compression measures and competing models.
3. Design and pilot the corpus and annotation scheme.
4. Test annotation reliability before large-scale extraction.
5. Build the balanced corpus.
6. Run exploratory analysis.
7. Freeze a confirmatory specification and test it.
8. Publish negative and robustness results alongside positive findings.
