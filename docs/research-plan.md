# Research Plan

## Phase 0 — Literature autopsy

Determine what is already known about conceptual engineering as engineering, inferential compression, implementation/uptake, formal models, and empirical corpus methods.

**Deliverable:** literature map and bibliography with methodological dependencies.

## Phase 1 — Theory and operationalization

Define candidate meanings of normative compression. Build competing models. Specify observable variables without assuming the target result.

**Deliverable:** frozen pilot codebook and candidate metrics.

## Phase 2 — Pilot corpus

Construct a small balanced corpus and manually annotate claims/arguments. Test whether independent annotators can identify the relevant components.

**Deliverable:** reliability report and revised codebook.

## Phase 3 — Model validation

Generate synthetic arguments with known compression properties. Test whether graph/information metrics recover the known structure.

**Deliverable:** simulation notebook and metric-selection report.

## Phase 4 — Corpus construction

Build the large balanced corpus using pre-specified sampling rules. Preserve provenance and metadata.

**Deliverable:** versioned corpus and sampling report.

## Phase 5 — Exploratory analysis

Search broadly for patterns, including results that undermine the hypothesis. Exploration may modify hypotheses but does not count as confirmatory evidence.

**Deliverable:** exploratory report and preregistration specification.

## Phase 6 — Confirmatory analysis

Run the frozen analysis on held-out data. Compare the normative-compression model against alternatives.

**Deliverable:** statistical/model results and figures.

## Phase 7 — Robustness and replication

Repeat with alternative definitions, strata, terms, annotators, and models. Publish negative results.

**Deliverable:** robustness report.

## Phase 8 — Philosophical interpretation

Only after empirical results are established, return to conceptual engineering, Rorty, normative theory, and the substantive Indigenous case.

**Deliverable:** philosophical argument constrained by the empirical findings.
